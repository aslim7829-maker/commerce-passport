The core idea

A purchase shouldn't disappear after the receipt is printed.

Today, a product's commercial identity is scattered across paper receipts, WhatsApp chats, seller systems, warranty cards and service centres. Commerce Passport connects those events into one permissioned object.

01

Receipt → identity
Every verified purchase becomes a durable record instead of a disposable transaction.

02

Identity → service
The product can carry warranty, repair and installation context into future interactions.

03

Service → resale
Ownership and service history can become portable proof for a future buyer, insurer or trade-in provider.

One object, many events

The passport follows the product through its lifecycle.

The first MVP should focus on durable goods where warranty and service friction is high: electronics, appliances, vehicles, equipment and other ownership-heavy purchases.

One API

A simple event layer can sit between your POS, ecommerce platform, service centre and the customer's passport.

purchase.createdCreates or updates a product record.
warranty.updatedAttaches coverage and expiry data.
service.completedAdds verified service history.
ownership.transferredMoves ownership permission.
return.acceptedLinks reverse logistics to the object.
recall.matchedFlags affected product identities.

FAQ

What this is — and what it isn't.

No. The concept is a commerce identity layer for purchases, assets and services. It should not become a government identity document and should collect the minimum information required for commerce use cases.

The product should be designed around customer-controlled permissions. A seller can verify a purchase, while a technician might receive only the information needed to perform a service.

The first version can be extremely lightweight: scan a product/serial, enter the customer Commerce ID, and issue a digital receipt link. A deep POS integration can come later.

Durable goods with meaningful warranties and service costs are a strong wedge: electronics, appliances, phones, laptops, vehicle-related equipment and business machinery.
